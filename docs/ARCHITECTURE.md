# Architecture — the decisions and why

Not a diagram walkthrough; module docstrings do that. This is the set of
choices somebody could reasonably have made differently, and the reason we
did not.

---

## 1. The ledger is canonical; events are a view

**The decision.** Raw bytes are the source of truth. Normalized events are
derived, disposable, and re-derivable at any time.

**The alternative.** Treat the parsed event as the product and keep raw as an
optional backup — what essentially every log pipeline does.

**Why.** Three consequences fall out of the inversion, and none of them can be
retrofitted:

- Requirement (a) becomes structural. Losslessness is not a feature that could
  regress; it is what the store *is*.
- Requirement (d) is free. Every event names the bytes it came from because
  those bytes are still there.
- **Rewind exists.** Correcting a grammar re-derives history. A pipeline that
  discarded the raw cannot do this at any price.

**What it costs.** Storage. Roughly 1.1–1.3× compressed against the raw feed,
which is the price of being able to answer a question you had not thought of
yet.

---

## 2. Shape-first triage

**The decision.** One character-counting pass assigns a line to a structural
family; only same-family grammars are then evaluated.

**The alternative.** Score every grammar's rules against every line.

**Why.** The naive approach is O(grammars) per event, so onboarding sources
makes the pipeline slower — precisely backwards for a product whose promise is
easy onboarding. Structure is decidable far more cheaply than identity: a line
is JSON, or has 34 commas, or holds 12 `key=value` pairs, and one pass tells
you which.

**Measured.** 2.7 of 10 grammars evaluated per line; 73% of classification work
avoided. The fingerprint is computed once and reused by triage, the compiler and
the Forge.

---

## 3. Grammars are compiled, not interpreted

**The decision.** At load, a grammar becomes a flat list of closures with
arguments bound. Per event: `for fn in plan: fn(state)`.

**The alternative.** Walk the declared steps per event and branch on step type.

**Why.** The step type cannot change between events — only the data can. So the
branch is a decision re-made millions of times to reach an answer fixed at load
time. Everything hoistable is hoisted: regexes compiled, column names frozen
into tuples, OCSF paths pre-split into segments, cast functions resolved, enum
tables lower-cased.

**Measured.** The largest single throughput win in the parse layer, and free at
runtime because the work happens once per grammar.

---

## 4. Merkle trees rather than a hash chain

**The decision.** Each stratum is sealed with a Merkle tree; roots are chained
across strata.

**The alternative.** A pure hash chain over records.

**Why.** A chain proves the *whole archive* is unaltered and nothing less. To
convince a third party that one specific line is present, you must give them
every other line so they can recompute — often exactly what you cannot do, when
the other records belong to other cases or other customers.

A tree gives an inclusion proof in ~log₂(n) hashes. For 20,000 records that is
15 hashes: a few hundred bytes that prove membership and disclose nothing else.

**Details that matter.** Leaves and internal nodes are hashed with distinct
prefixes (RFC 6962) — without that, an internal node can be presented as a leaf
and a proof forged for data never stored. Odd nodes are *promoted*, not
duplicated; duplicating makes two distinct trees share a root, which is its own
forgery vector. Both are one-line decisions with real consequences, and both
have tests.

---

## 5. Slotted dataclasses, not a validation library, in the hot path

**The decision.** Internal objects are `@dataclass(slots=True)`. Pydantic is
used only at trust boundaries: grammar files and HTTP request bodies.

**Why.** These objects are constructed once per log line. A validating model
charges a schema walk per construction — to validate data we produced
ourselves, in-process, from code we control. `__slots__` additionally removes
the per-instance dict (~40% less memory per event) and turns an attribute typo
into an `AttributeError` rather than a silently-created field.

**Where validation does belong.** Grammar files are written by humans and
generated by the Forge; API bodies arrive over the network. Both are validated
strictly, and unknown keys are rejected rather than ignored — a silently
dropped `fileds:` means an author believes a mapping is live when it is not.

---

## 6. No message broker in the shipped stack

**The decision.** The pipeline consumes an iterator. There is no Kafka.

**Why.** The ledger already *is* a durable, ordered, append-only log with
replay semantics. Adding a broker means two append-only logs in one process,
one redundant, plus a daemon to operate, to air-gap, and to fail during a demo.

What a broker genuinely buys is decoupling *across machines*, which matters at
multi-node and not at one. So the seam is placed where it belongs: `Pipeline`
takes an iterator in and a list of outlets out. Swapping the in-process path
for a broker is an adapter, not a rewrite.

**When to add one.** Past roughly 4 nodes. Partition by source device, never
round-robin — the ledger is single-writer per shard, and per-source
partitioning also keeps one device's events ordered, which is what makes
session correlation tractable.

---

## 7. Hand-written timestamp parsers

**The decision.** Each supported format is a slicer reading digits at fixed
offsets, with separators checked.

**The alternative.** `datetime.strptime`.

**Why.** strptime re-parses its format string and rebuilds a regex on every
call, for the most repetitive operation in the system. Roughly an order of
magnitude slower.

**The part that is not about speed.** RFC 3164 syslog carries *no year and no
timezone*. Every implementation must invent both, and that is where real log
pipelines quietly go wrong — a December log read on 2 January lands twelve
months in the future. STRATA states the year rule explicitly (take the current
year; if that lands more than a day ahead, use the previous) and refuses to
guess the zone: a grammar must declare it. Declaring an assumption makes it
reviewable; inferring one makes it a bug nobody can see.

Named zones are rejected on purpose. A stored timestamp must not depend on a
tz-database version, or the same archive reads differently on two machines.

---

## 8. Single-writer ledger, enforced

**The decision.** One writer per shard, taken as an advisory `flock` at open,
raising `LedgerBusy` with a specific message.

**Why.** Concurrent appends to one segment interleave and corrupt frames. The
constraint is architectural, so it belongs at the door — before the lock
existed, a second process started happily and failed later, deep inside SQLite,
with "database is locked" surfacing as an anonymous 500 at request time. The
operator saw a broken server rather than the actual problem.

**Scale path.** More shards, each with one writer — exactly how it would work
across machines.

---

## 9. Batched durability

`fsync` plus `COMMIT` per record costs more than every other pipeline stage
combined. STRATA batches both (`sync_every`, default 2048), as every production
log system does. At most `sync_every` records are at risk in a hard power loss;
set it to 1 for per-record durability at roughly 20× less throughput.

**Losslessness is unaffected either way.** The bytes stored are identical; only
the moment they are forced to the platter moves.

---

## 10. Value-based inference in the Forge

**The decision.** Field semantics are inferred from *values and adjacency*, not
only from names.

**Why.** Name matching only works on sources considerate enough to label their
fields. Positional CSV has no names at all — and positional CSV is most of the
perimeter. Knowing a column is called `col_08` tells you nothing; knowing every
value in it is an IPv4 address, that there are 137 distinct ones, and that the
column two later is always an integer under 65536 tells you it is a source
endpoint and its port.

That is how a human reads an unfamiliar log. Every rule is a heuristic and can
be wrong, which is exactly why the Forge proposes and a human publishes.
